# RLJ Editors Guide

Editors are responsible for: (1) deciding whether to recommend desk-rejection of a
submission, (2) assigning (or recruiting) reviewers, (3) skimming the paper themselves,
(4) writing a meta-review, (5) recommending acceptance, revisions, or rejection, and
(6) in the case of revisions, handling the resubmission if it arrives within three
months. For the wider context, see the [RLJ Reviewers Guide](/reviewers-guide).

While the decision should be heavily informed by the reviews, note that Editors are
expected to skim submitted papers to inform their decisions. The depth of this skim (or
read) may scale based on the reviews. For example, if the reviews overwhelmingly
recommend rejection, the Editor may skim to confirm these reasons for rejection.
Thorough reads are primarily necessary for borderline cases.

## Sequence of Steps

When assigned a paper, editors should perform the following sequence of steps.

### 1. Desk rejection assessment

Perform a quick read of the paper to assess whether it should be desk-rejected.
Criteria for desk rejection (and a description of the expected desk rejection text) can
be found in [Desk Rejection Criteria and Process](/editors-guide/desk-rejection).

### 2. Assign and recruit reviewers

Assign a Senior Reviewer and a Technical Reviewer in OpenReview. If you know of
particularly suitable reviewers for the paper who are not already in the system, you

just this one paper, without joining the RLJ reviewer pool. You should complete reviewer 
assignment within 2 weeks of being assigned the paper. This step is critical - 
identifying or recruiting reliable reviewers whom you trust will make your subsequent
job much easier.

### 3. Obtain reviews

Wait for the reviewers to complete their reviews, within 3 weeks. You are encouraged
to send reminder emails (including outside OpenReview) as the deadline approaches to
ensure reviews arrive on time. If reviewers are irresponsible, report them and replace 
them with new reviewers. These reports are critical to the functioning of the review 
process, and the Editors-in-Chief are committed to acting on your reports.

Ensure that you are familiar with the
[Senior Reviewer](/reviewers-guide/senior-reviewers) and
[Technical Reviewer](/reviewers-guide/technical-reviewers) instructions. If reviewers fail to
follow the instructions, work with them to revise their reviews following the
instructions.

## Accept / Revisions / Reject Criteria

Review all of the other reference pages, particularly
[Supporting Claims](/reviewers-guide/supporting-claims),
[Desk Rejection Criteria and Process](/editors-guide/desk-rejection), and
[Polished and Well Written](/reviewers-guide/polished). These should be your primary resource
for expectations regarding the acceptance criteria for RLJ.

Here we re-emphasize that RLJ aims to have the highest bar for scientific rigor. We
fully support the rejection of papers that have errors in scientific methodology or
reporting, and which therefore do not sufficiently support their claims. Examples
include failure to report the number of trials or seeds in experiments, the omission of
discussion of how hyperparameters were tuned, failure to report the meaning of error bar
widths, and failing to state implicit assumptions in theorems.

Note that this applies even if these issues could be fixed with minor edits. That is,
such errors are viewed as a significant issue that typically results in rejection,
not revisions. Note that rejection comes with a 6-month wait to resubmit, while revision
allows immediate resubmission. *This deliberately strict policy is designed to encourage
authors to carefully check the validity of their work, rather than relying on you and
the review process to catch their mistakes.*

We also re-emphasize that RLJ aims to avoid tastemaking. Acceptance should not be
based on the perceived impact of the work, nor should it be based on whether one wishes
the authors made different claims from the ones they chose (e.g., if the authors don't
claim state-of-the-art performance, then a lack of state-of-the-art performance is not a
reason for rejection). When incremental papers are rejected (by first discussing with an
Editor-in-Chief for extreme cases), it should typically be because they over-claim in an
attempt to appear more significant than they are. If authors are up-front and clear
about the incremental nature of their work (and the limitations of the clearly claimed
contributions), the work should still be accepted to RLJ, assuming there are no other
issues.

## Meta-Review Format

The meta-review need not be a full-length review (you can write one if you want). Note
that your meta-review should primarily be phrased in terms of the claimed contributions
on the cover page, whether their scope is appropriate, and whether they are properly
contextualized. This is something more senior researchers (like you!) are much better
positioned to do.

## Revision Process

If the paper receives "Revisions", the authors will be encouraged to resubmit within 3
months. In such cases, ensure that the meta-review clearly states what issues the
authors need to address for their work to be accepted.

If the authors do resubmit, their resubmission should come with a letter to you, the
Editor, that discusses the primary concerns that you and the reviewers had with the
initial submission, and how any changes to their submission address them. Note that
authors may disagree with concerns that were raised, and this should be discussed in the
letter even if the authors choose not to revise the manuscript.

You may then decide whether to accept or reject the paper immediately, whether to
consult the previous reviewers, or whether to initiate an entirely new review of the
paper. Barring extenuating circumstances, resubmitted papers should either be accepted
or rejected — they should not be returned for further revisions.
