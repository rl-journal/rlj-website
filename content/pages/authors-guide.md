# Authors Guide

This guide covers how to prepare your manuscript file: formatting, page limits, the
cover page, and what to submit after acceptance. For scope, originality, disclosure and
the review process, see [RLJ Submission Guidelines](/submit).

## Formatting Instructions

All submissions should be in PDF format. All of the content before (but not including)
the references is referred to as the **main text**. There is a recommended page limit of
**8 pages** and a strict page limit of **12 pages** for the main text; authors should use
their best judgment to edit the paper to ensure the content needed to understand the main
parts of the method is included in the main text. The cover page does not count towards
the 8–12-page limit.

Main texts exceeding 8 pages are reserved for papers that cannot fully convey their core
scientific contributions within 8 pages (e.g., for clear and thorough proofs or plots
related to additional experimentation). Reviewers may penalize papers for unnecessary
main text length above 8 pages, so authors are advised to minimize length while
maintaining scientific rigor.

Authors should look at previously published [RLJ papers](/volumes) for examples of
expectations on writing quality and organization. You must use the provided
[style files](/authors-guide/style-files). Any submissions that violate the style guidelines
may be rejected without further review.

## Cover Page

At RLJ, the first page of the PDF file should be a one-page cover page. In addition to
the title and abstract, the cover page must include a box clearly stating the paper's
main contributions.

*Submissions will be judged primarily on the contributions claimed on their cover pages
and the evidence provided to support those claims.* Major contributions should not be
claimed in the main text if they do not appear on the cover page. Overclaiming can lead
to a submission being rejected, so it is important to have well-scoped contribution
statements on the cover page.

Additionally, contribution statements should be contextualized and clearly (and briefly)
state the state of the field before the submission. The work discussed in the
contextualization needs to be relevant to the submission.

Reviewers use the cover page as a rubric—see
[Supporting Claims](/reviewers-guide/supporting-claims) for what constitutes sufficient support
for a claimed contribution.

## Supplementary Material

You are welcome to include supplementary materials alongside your paper submission.
These can include code, extra figures, and videos. It is crucial, however, that the main
text stands on its own as a complete document. Please note that reviewers are not obliged
to examine the supplementary materials or any text beyond the main text. Therefore, any
results critical to your paper should be fully detailed in the main text.

Note: the main text ends when the references begin. Appendices before the references are 
treated as part of the main text: subject to the 8–12 page limit, peer-reviewed, and may 
contain content central to the paper's claims. Supplementary material after the references
is not part of the main text, has no page limit, is not necessarily reviewed, and should not 
contain any claims or material central to the paper.

We recommend including all parts (main text, appendices, references, and supplementary
material) in a single PDF file.

## Preparing the Final Version

Upon paper acceptance, authors will be asked to submit a final version of the paper
generated using the `[accept]` option in the style files. At this stage, only minor
revisions (e.g., correcting typographical errors) will be permitted. Along with the 
final PDF, authors must also submit a publication form acknowledging the
rights granted to the journal—see
[Style Files and Forms](/authors-guide/style-files).

Copyright remains with the authors, who are free to share the final version of their
work. See [Publication](/submit#publication) for the licensing terms.
