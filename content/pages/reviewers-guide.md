# RLJ Reviewers Guide

> *The RLJ peer review process prioritizes rigorous methodology over perceived
> importance, aiming to foster scholarly discussions on both well-established and
> emerging topics in RL.*

RLJ uses a reviewing process with **editors** and two types of reviewers: **senior
reviewers (SRs)** and **technical reviewers (TRs)**. The purpose of this guide
is to make each of these roles clear and to describe how to write each type of review.
It is worth stressing that this is notably different from the typical
NeurIPS/ICLR/ICML review process.

This guide is public. It covers the criteria a paper is judged against and how
each kind of review is written; the mechanics of the process — timeline, decisions,
resubmission — live on [Author Guide](/submit#review-process) so that they
are stated in one place. Authors are strongly encouraged to read both before submitting.

At a high level, the two types of reviews differ as follows. A **technical review** is
more or less a list of technical errors in the paper. There is no commentary on
novelty, significance, or impact. This is not an oversight on our part; it is very
intentional. A **senior review** is a typical review, but with a focus on claims
matching evidence while still including comments on technical correctness.

Editors correspond to the previous Senior Area Chair (SAC) and Area Chair (AC) roles,
and senior reviewers correspond to the previous Holistic Reviewers (HR).
Editors will find their own per-stage duties and deadlines in the
[Editors Guide](/editors-guide).

## How Papers are Evaluated

Acceptance is based only on the submission, not on the potential of the paper with
further edits. A paper that requires even minor changes may be desk rejected or
returned for revisions.

The criteria for acceptance of a paper into RLJ are:

1. **The paper clearly describes its claimed contributions.**
2. **The contributions are of interest to the RL community.**
3. **The paper provides sufficient support for its claimed contributions.**
4. **The paper does not contain other errors.**
5. **The paper is polished and well written.**

Below we describe each of these criteria in more detail.

### Criterion 1: The paper clearly describes its claimed contributions

All major contributions should be listed on the cover page. Additional claims are
unavoidable, but all major contributions should be listed on the cover page. A claim
not appearing on the cover page still needs to be sufficiently supported. The claims
themselves should be precise (not ambiguous or unclear).

### Criterion 2: The contributions are of interest to the RL community

As a general rule, there should be at least five people (other than the authors) who
read RLJ (or attend RLC) and who would be interested in reading the paper. This allows
for papers to be published in RLJ containing research that one may not view as RL
research, if the research is of interest to the RL community. However, papers must be
written with the RL community as the intended audience.

### Criterion 3: The paper provides sufficient support for its claimed contributions

See [Supporting Claims](/reviewers-guide/supporting-claims) for a description of what does and
does not constitute "sufficient support".

### Criterion 4: The paper does not contain other errors

Other errors that do not impact whether or not the paper provides sufficient support
for its claims can preclude acceptance, but typically only motivate the need for
revisions, not rejection.

### Criterion 5: The paper is polished and well written

A submission that is not polished and well written can result in rejection or
revisions, depending on the scale of the issues. See
[Polished and Well Written](/reviewers-guide/polished) for a description of what does and does
not constitute a polished and well-written paper.
